Goal

Close a full quarter by hand, with every number traceable

The goal was to take one small business through the full accounting cycle: from raw source documents to a Trial Balance, financial statements and a list of questions for the client. I wanted to show not just the result but how every number gets there.

Why Google Sheets and not QuickBooks

Every calculation is visible

In a spreadsheet each balance comes from a formula you can click and trace back to the journal entry and the source document. In accounting software most of that is hidden behind the interface.

The mechanics, not the buttons

Posting every entry through debits and credits by hand, like T-accounts, makes sure I understand what each transaction does to the books, rather than relying on the software to decide.

Schedules built from scratch

FIFO inventory and COGS, straight-line depreciation, loan amortization and payroll accruals were each calculated in their own schedule, so the adjusting entries can be checked line by line.

Easy to review

Anyone can open the link and check the work. No QuickBooks subscription or access to a company file is needed.

Errors don't hide

Without bank feed rules or auto-categorization nothing is posted silently. An unbalanced entry breaks the Trial Balance, and a wrong match shows up as an open vendor or customer balance.

The base for QuickBooks work

Knowing what the software does under the hood makes QuickBooks faster to use and easier to clean up. A follow-up project in QuickBooks Online is in progress.

Method

Every vendor and customer payment through A/P and A/R

Every vendor payment went through Accounts Payable and every customer payment through Accounts Receivable, even when there was no matching bill or invoice.

Once a bill meets its payment, the vendor balance nets to zero. The same goes for customers. Anything left over is a signal: a missing receipt, a short payment or a posting error.

That turns reconciliation into a simple check: scan the open balances by name. I used them to build the client request: 9 payments with missing receipts and the customers who still owe money.

Payroll, loans, the rent deposit, credit card payoffs and owner transactions went to their own accounts, since they are not vendor bills.

The tricky parts

Two places where it is easy to double-count

Credit card payoffs

A payoff shows up twice: as money leaving the bank and as money arriving on the card. I recorded it at the bank stage, so from the card statement I posted only the charges. Posting the payoff again would double it.

Paper checks

Checks go to Undeposited Funds first, not straight to the bank. One check arrived before its invoice was issued. That is a customer prepayment, so it went to Unearned Revenue, not A/R.
