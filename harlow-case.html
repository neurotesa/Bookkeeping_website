The client

Six months of books for a service company

Harlow Tax & Advisory provides bookkeeping and tax services to small businesses in Dallas, Texas. Six months of transactions: bank account, business credit card, vendor bills and customer invoices.

The task was to record January–June in QuickBooks Online, reconcile the bank as of June 30 and prepare the statements and the sales tax liability.

Method

Every vendor and customer payment through A/P and A/R

Every vendor payment went through Accounts Payable and every customer payment through Accounts Receivable, even when there was no bill or invoice yet.

Once a bill meets its payment, the vendor balance nets to zero. The same goes for customers. Anything left over is a signal: a missing bill, an unused credit or a payment nobody explained.

Undeposited Funds only for paper checks. When a customer payment arrived net of a bank fee, the payment was recorded in full and the fee went to Bank Expenses.

The tricky parts

Six places where the numbers can go wrong

Deposit before the invoice

A customer paid 3,000 before the work was done. That is a liability, not income, so it went to Deferred Payments. When the invoices were issued, a journal entry moved it to A/R: Dr Deferred Payments 3,000, Cr A/R 3,000.

Overpayments refunded

The same customer overpaid two invoices by 295 in total. The refunds, 120 and a 175 check, were posted against A/R, so the customer balance returned to zero and income was not reduced twice.

Credit memo with sales tax

The customer did not accept 5 hours of May work. The credit memo for 425 plus 8.25% tax, 460.06 in total, reduced both revenue and the tax owed to the Texas Comptroller.

Billable expense, counted once

Flight tickets for 650 were paid by the company and re-billed to the customer. A bank rule had already posted the same charge to Fuel. I undid it and matched the bank line to the billable expense, otherwise the cost would be counted twice.

Returned check

A 3,000 customer check bounced. I reopened the balance in A/R for 3,000 and posted the 35 bank fee to Bank Expenses as a separate transaction. The invoice is open again until the customer pays.

Bad debt write-off

The owner decided not to collect 1,500. That amount already included 8.25% sales tax, so the credit memo split it: 1,385.68 to Bad Debts and 114.32 back out of the tax owed to the Texas Comptroller.
